# Chest & Back — Pectoralis, Latissimus, Trapezius & Scapular Muscles

## Anatomy Overview

### Pectoralis Major

**Origin:** Clavicular head — medial clavicle; Sternocostal head — sternum, costal cartilage 1-6

**Insertion:** Lateral lip of bicipital groove

**Innervation:** Medial and lateral pectoral nerves (C5-T1)

Broad, fan-shaped muscle. Horizontal adduction (clavicular head) and flexion (sternocostal head). EMG: 80-100% MVIC during bench press, 70-90% during push-ups. [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/shoulder-girdle]] (pec major works with anterior deltoid for horizontal adduction).

### Pectoralis Minor

**Origin:** Ribs 3-5

**Insertion:** Coracoid process of scapula

**Innervation:** Medial and lateral pectoral nerves (C5-T1)

Thin, triangular muscle beneath pec major. Pulls scapula forward and down (protraction + depression). Primary driver of rounded shoulder posture when short/adaptive. [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/core]] (pec minor position linked to thoracic posture and breathing mechanics).

### Latissimus Dorsi

**Origin:** T7-L5 spinous processes, thoracolumbar fascia, iliac crest, ribs 9-12

**Insertion:** Floor of bicipital groove

**Innervation:** Thoracodorsal nerve (C6-C8)

Broad, triangular muscle — largest in back. Shoulder extensor, adductor, internal rotator. Primary muscle for pull-ups, rows, and pulldowns. Force couple with pec major for chest fly movements. [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/upper-arm]] (biceps shares insertion groove, acts as elbow flexor during pulling).

### Trapezius — Upper

**Origin:** External occipital protuberance, medial third superior nuchal line, ligamentum nuchae

**Insertion:** Lateral third of clavicle, acromion

**Innervation:** Spinal accessory nerve (CN XI); C3-C4 sensory

Scapular elevator + upward rotator. Sustained activity during overhead work and computer use → overactivity common. [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/shoulder-girdle]] (upper trap + middle/lower trap force couple for upward rotation).

### Trapezius — Middle

**Origin:** Spinous processes C7-T3

**Insertion:** Medial margin of scapular spine

**Innervation:** Spinal accessory nerve (CN XI)

Horizontal scapular retractor. Works with rhomboids for pure retraction. EMG: 60-80% MVIC during barbell rows. [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/hip]] (scapular position affects thoracic extension and posture).

### Trapezius — Lower

**Origin:** Spinous processes T4-T12

**Insertion:** Medial scapular spine at deltoid tubercle

**Innervation:** Spinal accessory nerve (CN XI)

Scapular depressor + upward rotator. Most underdeveloped trapezius division. Force couple with serratus anterior for scapular upward rotation. Prone Y raise → 80-100% MVIC.

### Rhomboid Major & Minor

**Origin:** Minor — nuchal ligament, C7-T1 spinous processes; Major — T2-T5 spinous processes

**Insertion:** Medial scapular border (minor above spine, major below)

**Innervation:** Dorsal scapular nerve (C5)

Scapular retractor + internal rotator. Works with middle trapezius for retraction. Active during rowing. Weakness → winged scapula.

### Levator Scapulae

**Origin:** Transverse processes C1-C4

**Insertion:** Superior angle of scapula

**Innervation:** Dorsal scapular nerve (C5) + C3-C4

Scapular elevator. Contributes to cervical spine strain when overactive. Often overactive in individuals with poor neck posture.

### Serratus Anterior

**Origin:** Lateral surface of ribs 1-8

**Insertion:** Medial scapular border (anterior surface)

**Innervation:** Long thoracic nerve (C5-C7)

Scapular protactor. Force couple with lower trapezius for upward rotation. Weakness → winged scapula (medial border lifts off thoracic wall). Push-up plus → 70-90% MVIC.

## Function in Exercise & Running

### Chest in Training

- **Bench press (all variations):** 80-100% MVIC; declines and inclines shift emphasis
- **Push-ups:** 70-85% MVIC; wider grip → more pectoral
- **Chest flyes:** 70-90% MVIC; pure horizontal adduction
- **Dips:** 80-95% MVIC (pec major emphasis with forward lean)

### Back in Training

- **Pull-ups / chin-ups:** 70-90% MVIC (lat); 60-80% (biceps)
- **Barbell rows:** 70-85% MVIC (lat + middle trap + rhomboid)
- **Lat pulldowns:** 70-85% MVIC (lat emphasis)
- **Seated cable rows:** 65-80% MVIC; scapular retraction component
- **Face pulls:** 60-75% MVIC; posterior deltoid + middle trap

### Trapezius in Training

- **Shrugs:** Upper trap dominant; 80-100% MVIC
- **Prone Y/T/W raises:** Lower/middle/upper trap isolation in prone
- **Scapular retraction holds:** Middle trap; isometric 60-80% MVIC
- **Wall slides:** Lower trap + serratus anterior coordination

### Running Mechanics

- **Arm swing:** Lat + pec major force couple for sagittal plane swing
- **Scapular position:** Trapezius maintains scapular stability during arm swing
- **Posture:** Thoracic extensors maintain upright position against hip flexor dominance
- **Breathing:** Pec minor/serratus role in costal expansion

## Common Injuries

| Structure | Injury | Mechanism |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Pec major | Tear / rupture | Eccentric overload; bench press bottom position |
| Pec minor | Shortening | Chronic postural adaptation; rounded shoulders |
| Latissimus | Tendinopathy | Overhead overuse; rowing/pulldown overload |
| Upper trapezius | Overactivity / strain | Computer use; stress; elevated shoulders |
| Middle trapezius | Weakness | Sedentary; push-dominant training |
| Lower trapezius | Weakness | Most commonly underdeveloped division |
| Rhomboids | Strain / trigger points | Scapular retraction overload; poor posture |
| Serratus anterior | Weakness / winging | Unopposed retraction; disuse atrophy |

**Winged scapula** — serratus anterior or trapezius weakness allows medial scapular border to lift off thoracic wall. Often from long thoracic nerve injury (C5-C7).

**Snapping scapula syndrome** — bursitis between scapula and thoracic wall; often associated with scapular dyskinesis.

**Thoracic outlet syndrome** — neurovascular compression from pec minor short/tight or cervical rib.

## Training Recommendations

### Balanced Chest-Back Program

- **Push:pull ratio:** 1:1 minimum; 1:2 for shoulder health
- **Row priority:** Compound rows early in session before pressing
- **Scapular control:** Retraction before protraction for all exercises
- **Unilateral work:** Single-arm rows to identify and correct imbalances

### Trapezius-Specific Training

- **Upper trap management:** Reduce shrug volume; focus on depression cue ("push chest up, shoulders down")
- **Middle trap emphasis:** Prone retraction holds; band pull-aparts; face pulls
- **Lower trap priority:** Prone Y raises (most evidence); wall slides; push-up plus

### Scapular Dyskinesis Correction

1. **Phase 1 (weeks 1-4):** Quadruped scapular clocks; wall slides; band pull-aparts
2. **Phase 2 (weeks 4-8):** Prone Y holds; serratus punches; low-load rows
3. **Phase 3 (weeks 8-12):** Loaded Y/T; cable rows with scapular emphasis
4. **Phase 4 (week 12+):** Integrated compound movements; sport-specific patterns
