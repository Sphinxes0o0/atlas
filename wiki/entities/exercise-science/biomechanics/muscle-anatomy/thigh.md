# Thigh — Quadriceps, Hamstrings & Adductors

## Anatomy Overview

### Quadriceps Group

**Rectus Femoris:** Origin — anterior inferior iliac spine, acetabular rim; Insertion — tibial tuberosity via patellar tendon. Only quad muscle crossing hip AND knee (hip flexion + knee extension). Innervation: Femoral nerve (L2-L4).

**Vastus Lateralis:** Origin — greater trochanter, lateral linea aspera; Insertion — tibial tuberosity. Largest quad muscle by mass. Lateral quadriceps dominance → patellar tracking issues.

**Vastus Medialis:** Origin — medial linea aspera; Insertion — tibial tuberosity. Distal fibers (VMO) attach to patella directly — critical for patellar stability. Atrophies rapidly post-knee injury.

**Vastus Intermedius:** Origin — anterior/lateral femoral shaft; Insertion — tibial tuberosity. Deepest quad muscle. Pure knee extensor.

**Innervation:** Femoral nerve (L2-L4) for all four.

[[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/hip]] (rectus femoris crosses hip joint; iliopsoas is antagonist).

### Hamstrings Group

**Biceps Femoris (long head):** Origin — ischial tuberosity; Insertion — fibular head. Hip extensor + knee flexor + external rotator of tibia. Innervation: Sciatic nerve, tibial portion (L5-S2).

**Biceps Femoris (short head):** Origin — lateral linea aspera; Insertion — fibular head. Knee flexor only (does NOT cross hip). Innervation: Sciatic nerve, common peroneal portion (L5-S2).

**Semitendinosus:** Origin — ischial tuberosity; Insertion — medial tibia (pes anserinus). Hip extensor + knee flexor + internal rotator of tibia. Innervation: Sciatic nerve, tibial portion (L5-S2).

**Semimembranosus:** Origin — ischial tuberosity; Insertion — medial tibial condyle. Hip extensor + knee flexor + internal rotator of tibia. Innervation: Sciatic nerve, tibial portion (L5-S2).

[[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/hip]] (gluteus maximus is primary hip extensor; hamstrings are secondary hip extensors).

### Adductor Group

**Adductor Magnus:** Origin — inferior pubic ramus, ischial ramus; Insertion — linea aspera, adductor tubercle. Primary adductor + hip extensor (posterior fibers). Most powerful adductor. Innervation: Obturator nerve (L2-L4) + sciatic (tibial portion).

**Adductor Longus:** Origin — body of pubis; Insertion — linea aspera. Pure adductor. Most common site of adductor strain.

**Adductor Brevis:** Origin — inferior pubic ramus; Insertion — pectineal line, linea aspera. Adductor + weak hip flexor.

**Pectineus:** Origin — pectineal line of pubis; Insertion — pectineal line of femur. Adduction + flexion. Innervation: Femoral + obturator nerves (L2-L4).

**Gracilis:** Origin — inferior pubic ramus; Insertion — medial tibia (pes anserinus). Adduction + knee flexion. Longest strap muscle.

[[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/core]] (adductor anatomy relates to pelvic floor; shared fascia with obliques).

## Function in Exercise & Running

### Quadriceps EMG During Key Exercises

|----------|----------------------|
| Back squat | 70-95% MVIC |
| Front squat | 80-100% MVIC |
| Leg press | 70-90% MVIC |
| Leg extension | 80-100% MVIC (isolation) |
| Hack squat | 75-95% MVIC |
| Running (late stance) | 50-70% MVIC |
| Walking uphill | 40-60% MVIC |

### Hamstrings EMG During Key Exercises

| Exercise | Hamstrings Activation |
|----------|----------------------|
| Romanian deadlift | 70-95% MVIC |
| Nordic curl | 80-100% MVIC |
| Leg curl | 70-90% MVIC |
| Running (late swing) | 50-70% MVIC |
| Hip thrust | 50-70% MVIC |

### VMO vs VL Balance

Vastus medialis obliquus (VMO) is functionally distinct from vastus medialis longus. VMO:
- Attaches directly to patella
- Angle of fibers (55° from vertical) pulls patella medially
- Must be specifically targeted in rehabilitation
- Weakness → lateral patellar tracking → anterior knee pain

### Thigh Muscle Force Coupling

- **Knee extension:** Quadriceps (all 4 heads) vs hamstrings (knee flexion component)
- **Hip extension:** Gluteus maximus + hamstrings vs iliopsoas
- **Hip adduction:** Adductor group vs gluteus medius/minimus (abduction)
- **Frontal plane:** Gluteus medius vs TFL vs adductors

### Running

- **Stance phase:** Quadriceps eccentrically absorb ground reaction; gluteus maximus extends hip
- **Swing phase:** Hamstrings eccentrically control knee extension; biceps femoris decelerates leg
- **Late swing:** Hamstrings pre-activate to prepare for ground contact

## Common Injuries

| Structure | Injury | Mechanism |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Quadriceps | Strain / contusion | Eccentric overload; direct impact |
| VMO | Weakness / atrophy | Post-knee surgery; patellofemoral pain |
| Rectus femoris | Strain / avulsion | Sprinting; hip flexor contraction; avulsion in adolescents |
| Hamstrings | Strain (biceps femoris) | Sprinting; late swing phase; most common strain site |
| Hamstrings | Tendinopathy (proximal) | Deadlift; hip extension overload |
| Adductor | Groin strain | Rapid adduction against resistance; change of direction |
| Adductor | Sports hernia ( Gilmore's) | Repetitive shear at pubic symphysis |

**Hamstring strain** — most common muscle strain in sports. Biceps femoris long head most frequently involved. Risk factors: prior hamstring strain, hamstring weakness, inadequate warm-up, fatigue.

**Quadriceps contusion** — direct blow to anterior thigh. Can cause significant hematoma and immobilization.

**Adductor strain** — common in hockey, soccer, rugby. Adductor longus most common site. Requires eccentric adductor strengthening for return to sport.

**Patellofemoral pain syndrome** — VMO weakness + VL dominance → lateral patellar tracking. Common in runners and jumping athletes.

## Training Recommendations

### Quadriceps Hypertrophy

- **Compound first:** Back squat (70-85% 1RM) or front squat; leg press for isolation
- **Load range:** 65-85% 1RM for 8-12 reps; lower for strength (3-6 reps)
- **VMO emphasis:** Terminal knee extensions (full extension hold); single-leg press
- **Frequency:** 2-3×/week; 48-72h recovery
- **VMO cue:** "Squeeze kneecap toward hip pocket"

### Hamstrings Strength & Injury Prevention

**Eccentric focus:**
- Nordic curls: 3×5-8 (eccentric emphasis; learn pattern first)
- RDL variations: 3×6-10; hip hinge pattern mastery
- Sliders / leg curls: 3×10-12 eccentric
- Single-leg RDL: 3×8 per side

**Training ratio:**
- Nordic curl (concentric:eccentric = 1:3)
- Volume ratio: hamstrings should receive ~80% of quad volume for balanced knee health

### Adductor Training

- **Copenhagen adductor exercise:** 3×8-12 per side
- **Side-lying adduction:** 3×15-20
- **Sumo deadlift:** Adductor involvement in wide stance
- **Adductor stretch:** Lateral side lunge; 30-60s hold

### Running-Specific Thigh Training

- **Eccentric quad work:** Decline squat, eccentric leg press
- **Plyometrics:** Jump squats, bounding (for power runners)
- **Hill repeats:** Quadriceps and hamstrings eccentric loading
