# Upper Arm — Biceps, Triceps & Forearm Flexors

## Anatomy Overview

### Biceps Brachii

**Origin:** Short head — coracoid process of scapula; Long head — supraglenoid tubercle

**Insertion:** Radial tuberosity, bicipital aponeurosis

**Innervation:** Musculocutaneous nerve (C5-C6)

Two-headed muscle crossing both shoulder and elbow. Strong supinator (40% of supination torque) and elbow flexor. Long head tendon traverses the intertubercular groove — vulnerable to tendinopathy in overhead athletes. [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/shoulder-girdle]] (long head functions as shoulder stabilizer).

### Brachialis

**Origin:** Anterior humerus (distal 1/2)

**Insertion:** Coronoid process of ulna

**Innervation:** Musculocutaneous nerve (C5-C6)

Pure elbow flexor — lies deep to biceps. Generates more elbow flexion force than biceps.[[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/chest-back]] (brachialis anchored to ulna, decoupled from scapular movement).

### Brachioradialis

**Origin:** Lateral supracondylar ridge of humerus

**Insertion:** Styloid process of radius

**Innervation:** Radial nerve (C5-C6)

Neutral-grip elbow flexor. Flexes elbow most efficiently at 90° forearm pronation/supination. Zottman curl targets this muscle specifically. [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/lower-leg]] (radial nerve name shared but different branch —同名不同源).

### Triceps Brachii

**Origin:** Long head — infraglenoid tubercle; Lateral head — posterior humerus above radial groove; Medial head — posterior humerus below radial groove

**Insertion:** Olecranon process of ulna

**Innervation:** Radial nerve (C5-C6)

Three-headed muscle — only true elbow extensor. Long head also contributes to shoulder extension (unique among elbow extensors). [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/chest-back]] (latissimus dorsi is antagonist to long head at shoulder).

| Head | Function | EMG Notes |
|------|----------|-----------|
| Long | Elbow extension + shoulder extension (adduction) | 70-90% MVIC (close-grip bench) |
| Lateral | Elbow extension (most superficial) | 80-100% MVIC (push-downs) |
| Medial | Elbow extension (bulk of elbow extension torque) | Active at all loads |

### Anconeus

**Origin:** Lateral epicondyle of humerus

**Insertion:** Olecranon + proximal ulna

**Innervation:** Radial nerve (C5-C6)

Small triangular muscle — elbow extension synergist. Assists triceps in final 10-15° of extension. Also stabilizes elbow during pronation/supination.

### Pronator Teres & Supinator

**Pronator teres:** C5-C6 via median nerve. Forearm pronation + elbow flexion. Activated heavily during supinated curl (resists pronation).

**Supinator:** C5-C6 via radial nerve. Forearm supination + elbow flexion. Most powerful supinator; active during bicep curl eccentric phase.

## Function in Exercise & Running

### Biceps in Training

- **Bicep curl:** 70-85% MVIC; supinated grip isolates
- **Hammer curl:** Brachioradialis + brachialis emphasis; neutral grip
- **Chin-up:** 60-80% MVIC; elbow flexion + shoulder flexion component
- **Preacher curl:** Eliminates momentum; increased biceps stretch at bottom
- **Zottman curl:** Full pronation/supination cycle; targets brachioradialis

### Triceps in Training

- **Close-grip bench press:** Triceps emphasis; 80-95% MVIC
- **Skull crushers:** Long head stretch; 70-85% MVIC
- **Push-downs:** Lateral head emphasis; 80-100% MVIC
- **Overhead extension:** Long head emphasis (shoulder flexion position stretches long head)
- **Dips:** 70-90% MVIC; loaded stretch on all three heads

### Running Arm Swing

- Biceps decelerates elbow extension during forward arm swing
- Triceps decelerates elbow flexion during backward arm swing
- Neither primary energy generator — arm swing aids balance and running economy
- Forearm musculature maintains grip on handheld objects (water bottles, poles)

## Common Injuries

| Structure | Injury | Mechanism |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Biceps long head | Tendinopathy / SLAP tear | Repetitive overhead; deep flexion against resistance |
| Biceps | Distal biceps rupture | Forced eccentric contraction; "Popeye" deformity |
| Triceps | Tendinopathy / rupture | Overload; rare complete rupture |
| Brachialis | Strain / contusion | Direct impact; forced flexion against resistance |
| Forearm flexors | Epicondylitis (medial) | Repetitive wrist flexion; golfer's elbow |
| Radial nerve | Wrist drop | Compression; proximal humerus fracture |

**Bicipital tendinopathy** — pain over anterior shoulder/distal biceps groove. Worse with overhead activity and resisted supination.

**Lateral epicondylitis (tennis elbow)** — extensor carpi radialis brevis most commonly; overuse of wrist extension against resistance.

**Medial epicondylitis (golfer's elbow)** — pronator teres and flexor carpi radialis involvement; less common than lateral.

## Training Recommendations

### Biceps Hypertrophy

- **Frequency:** 2-3×/week; 48-72h between sessions
- **Rep range:** 8-15 reps for hypertrophy; lower for strength
- **Grip variation:** Supinated (curls) → neutral (hammer) → pronated (Zottman) cycle
- **Stretch emphasis:** Incline curls for长 headelongation; preacher curls for peak contraction
- **Chin-ups:** Include as compound pulling for functional biceps strength

### Triceps Hypertrophy

- **Frequency:** 2-3×/week; 48-72h recovery
- **Compound first:** Dips and close-grip bench press before isolation
- **Head-specific cues:**
  - Overhead extension → long head stretch
  - Push-downs → lateral head isolation
  - Skull crushers → all heads at end-range
- **Volume:** 3-5 sets per session across 2-3 exercises

### Forearm / Grip Training

- **Wrist curls:** Flexor mass; 3×12-15, moderate load
- **Reverse wrist curls:** Extensor mass; important for balance
- **Grippers / dead hangs:** Crush grip strength; 3-5 holds of max effort
- **Farmers walks:** Total forearm workload + core integration
