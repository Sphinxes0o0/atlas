# Shoulder Girdle — Deltoid, Rotator Cuff & Teres Major

## Anatomy Overview

The shoulder girdle comprises the muscles governing glenohumeral joint mechanics: the deltoid (prime mover of shoulder abduction), the four rotator cuff muscles (dynamic stabilizers), and the teres major (internal rotator/adductor). Together they form a force-couple system balancing mobility against stability.

### Deltoid

**Origin:** Lateral third of clavicle (anterior), acromion (middle), spine of scapula (posterior)

**Insertion:** Deltoid tuberosity of humerus

**Innervation:** Axillary nerve (C5-C6)

| Head | Primary Action | EMG Highlight |
|------|---------------|--------------|
| Anterior | Shoulder flexion, horizontal adduction | 70-90% MVIC (overhead press) |
| Middle | Shoulder abduction | 80-100% MVIC (lateral raise) |
| Posterior | Shoulder extension, horizontal abduction | 60-80% MVIC (reverse fly) |

### Rotator Cuff — Supraspinatus

**Origin:** Supraspinous fossa of scapula

**Insertion:** Superior facet of greater tuberosity

**Innervation:** Suprascapular nerve (C5-C6)

Initiates first 15° of shoulder abduction. Exposed to impingement beneath acromial arch. [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/upper-arm]] (biceps long head tendon runs through same corridor).

### Rotator Cuff — Infraspinatus

**Origin:** Infraspinous fossa

**Insertion:** Middle facet of greater tuberosity

**Innervation:** Suprascapular nerve (C5-C6)

Primary external rotator. Provides 60-70% of external rotation torque. Critical eccentric decelerator during throwing follow-through. [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/chest-back]] (latissimus dorsi works as antagonist/internal rotator).

### Rotator Cuff — Teres Minor

**Origin:** Lateral border of scapula (superior 2/3)

**Insertion:** Inferior facet of greater tuberosity

**Innervation:** Axillary nerve (C5-C6)

Contributes ~20-25% of external rotation torque. Works alongside infraspinatus; becomes sole external rotator in massive cuff tears. [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/chest-back]] (serratus anterior protraction-force couple).

### Rotator Cuff — Subscapularis

**Origin:** Subscapular fossa (anterior scapula)

**Insertion:** Lesser tuberosity

**Innervation:** Upper and lower subscapular nerves (C5-C6)

Largest rotator cuff muscle. Primary internal rotator. Provides anterior humeral head stabilization. [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/hip]] (iliopsoas shares C5-C6 innervation).

### Teres Major (Not Rotator Cuff)

**Origin:** Posterior surface of inferior scapular angle

**Insertion:** Medial lip of bicipital groove

**Innervation:** Lower subscapular nerve (C5-C6)

Internal rotation, adduction, extension. Distinct from teres minor — no rotator cuff function. [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/upper-arm]] (biceps shares bicipital groove region).

## Function in Exercise & Running

### Deltoid in Resistance Training

- **Overhead press:** Anterior + middle deltoid dominant; 70-90% MVIC
- **Lateral raises:** Middle deltoid primary; strict form avoids trapezius substitution
- **Reverse flyes / face pulls:** Posterior deltoid target; 60-80% MVIC
- **Upright row:** Middle deltoid + upper trapezius; narrow grip increases impingement risk

### Rotator Cuff Function

All four rotator cuff muscles co-contract to center the humeral head in the glenoid during upper limb loading. During running:

- **Arm swing:** Subscapularis decelerates internal rotation; infraspinatus/teres minor decelerate external rotation
- **Glenohumeral stability:** Rotator cuff compresses humeral head into glenoid (concavity-compression)
- **Scapulohumeral rhythm:** 2:1 humeral-to-scapular rotation ratio maintained by cuff force couples

### Teres Major in Training

- Cable/band internal rotation: 60-75% MVIC
- Chin-ups and pulldowns: Substantial involvement as shoulder adductor/extensor
- Rowing variations: 50-65% MVIC as synergist

## Common Injuries

| Structure | Injury | Mechanism |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Supraspinatus | Impingement / tear | Repetitive overhead; compressed under acromial arch |
| Infraspinatus | Tendinopathy | External rotation overuse; eccentric overload in throwing |
| Teres minor | Rare isolated injury | Typically involved in massive cuff tears |
| Subscapularis | Tear / tendinopathy | Internal rotation overload; latch-key lesion |
| Teres major | Tendinopathy | Overuse in rowing / pulling; insertional stress at bicipital groove |
| Deltoid | Strain / contusion | Direct trauma; excessive overload |

**Shoulder impingement syndrome** — rotator cuff tendons pinched under acromial arch. Presents as painful arc (60-120° abduction). Common in overhead athletes and swimmers.

**Rotator cuff tear** — acute traumatic or chronic degenerative. Supraspinatus most commonly affected. Presents with weakness, pain, and limited active ROM.

**Glenohumeral instability** — ligamentous laxity or rotator cuff weakness allowing excessive humeral head translation.

## Training Recommendations

### Rotator Cuff Strengthening

1. **Side-lying external rotation** — infraspinatus/teres minor: 3×12-15, light resistance
2. **Internal rotation** — subscapularis: 3×12-15, cable/band
3. **Full can test** (scaption with ER) — supraspinatus isolation at 30° abduction
4. **External rotation at 90° abduction** — emphasizes teres minor contribution
5. **Prone Y/T/W raises** — combined cuff + scapular stabilizer work

### Deltoid Hypertrophy

- **Frequency:** 2-3×/week with 48-72h recovery
- **Volume:** 3-5 sets per head; 8-15 reps for hypertrophy
- **Progressive overload:** Increase load when target reps achieved with good form
- **Mind-muscle cue:** "Squeeze the shoulder blade down and back" for posterior deltoid

### Balance Principles

- **Push:pull ratio:** 1:1 for shoulder health — include horizontal pulling (rows, face pulls)
- **ER:IR ratio:** Maintain ~1:3 ER:IR strength ratio; overhead athletes may need 1:1
- **Scapular control:** Ensure scapular retraction/depression before loading
