# Core — Abdominal Wall & Spinal Stabilizers

## Anatomy Overview

### Rectus Abdominis

**Origin:** Pubic crest and symphysis

**Insertion:** Xiphoid process and costal cartilage 5-7

**Innervation:** Intercostal nerves T7-T12

Segmented muscle running vertical along anterior abdomen. Primary trunk flexor. Active in all anterior chain exercises. EMG: 50-70% MVIC during crunch, 40-60% during plank, 70-90% during roll-out variations. [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/chest-back]] (pec minor and obliques share thoracic innervation).

### External Oblique

**Origin:** External surface of ribs 5-8

**Insertion:** Linea alba, pubic tubercle, anterior iliac crest

**Innervation:** Intercostal nerves T7-T12, iliohypogastric nerve (L1)

Superficial antero-lateral abdominal wall. Ipsilateral rotation (same-side rotation). Lateral flexion. Compresses abdomen. [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/hip]] (external oblique shares iliac crest attachment with gluteus medius fascia).

### Internal Oblique

**Origin:** Thoracolumbar fascia, iliac crest, inguinal ligament

**Insertion:** Ribs 10-12, linea alba

**Innervation:** Intercostal nerves T7-T12, iliohypogastric nerve (L1)

Deep to external oblique. Contralateral rotation (opposite-side rotation). Force couple with external oblique for rotational control. Works with transversus abdominis for abdominal compression.

### Transversus Abdominis

**Origin:** Internal surface of costal cartilage 7-12, thoracolumbar fascia

**Insertion:** Linea alba, pubic crest

**Innervation:** Intercostal nerves T7-T12, iliohypogastric nerve (L1)

Deepest abdominal muscle — horizontal fiber orientation. Primary trunk stabilizer; acts as "weight belt." Activation precedes limb movement ( feed-forward stabilization). [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/lower-leg]] (transversus functions analogously to soleus as anti-movement stabilizer).

### Erector Spinae Group

**Iliocostalis, longissimus, spinalis** — three columns running along vertebral column

**Origin:** Sacrum, iliac crest, spinous processes

**Insertion:** Ribs, transverse processes, spinous processes, occiput

**Innervation:** Dorsal rami of spinal nerves (C3-S3)

Primary spinal extensors. Active during hip hinge movements, deadlifts, good mornings. [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/hip]] (erector spinae shares thoracolumbar fascia with gluteus maximus — posterior chain connection).

### Deep Core — Multifidus

**Origin:** Sacrum, transverse processes

**Insertion:** Spinous processes (2-4 segments above)

**Innervation:** Dorsal rami of spinal nerves

Multisegmental paraspinal muscle. Individual vertebral level control. Primary stabilizer during limb loading. Atrophies rapidly with back pain.

### Deep Core — Quadratus Lumborum

**Origin:** Iliac crest, iliolumbar ligament

**Insertion:** 12th rib, transverse processes L1-L4

**Innervation:** Ventral rami T12-L3

Lateral spinal stabilizer. Unilateral activity → lateral flexion. Frontal plane stabilization during walking/running.

### Deep Core — Rotatores

**Origin:** Transverse processes

**Insertion:** Spinous process (1-2 segments above)

**Innervation:** Dorsal rami of spinal nerves

Smallest deep paraspinal muscles. Segment-to-segment rotational control. Present at all spinal levels but largest in thoracic region.

## Function in Exercise & Running

### Abdominal EMG During Common Exercises

| Exercise | Rectus Abdominis | External Oblique | Internal Oblique | Transversus |
|----------|-----------------|------------------|-----------------|-------------|
| Crunch | 50-70% | 30-40% | 30-50% | 20-30% |
| Plank | 40-60% | 40-60% | 50-70% | 50-70% |
| Dead bug | 50-70% | 40-60% | 60-80% | 70-90% |
| Pallof press | 30-50% | 50-70% | 60-80% | 60-80% |
| Roll-out | 70-90% | 40-60% | 40-60% | 30-50% |

### Core in Running

- **Stabilization:** Trunk stabilizes against hip flexion/extension forces
- **Force transfer:** Transversus and obliques transfer force between upper and lower body
- **Lateral stability:** QL and obliques maintain frontal plane stability during single-leg support
- **Breathing:** Diaphragm + transversus abdominis coordination for intra-abdominal pressure

### Spinal Stabilization Hierarchy

1. **Transversus abdominis** — 30-40% MVIC during trunk perturbation tasks
2. **Multifidus** — Pre-activates 20-50ms before limb movement
3. **Diaphragm** — Inspiration-related increase in intra-abdominal pressure
4. **Pelvic floor** — Often neglected but integral to core stability system

## Common Injuries

| Structure | Injury | Mechanism |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Rectus abdominis | Strain / rectus diastasis | Overload of trunk flexion; postpartum midline separation |
| External / internal oblique | Strain | Rotational overload; sports with rapid rotation |
| Transversus abdominis | Inhibition | Pain-induced shutdown; disuse atrophy |
| Erector spinae | Strain | Hip hinge overload; deadlift with poor form |
| Multifidus | Atrophy | Back pain; rapid disuse following injury |
| Quadratus lumborum | Trigger points / strain | Lateral flexion overuse; asymmetric loading |

**Rectus diastasis** — separation of left/right rectus along linea alba. Common postpartum; also in heavy lifters. Measured as finger-widths of gap during crunch.

**Low back pain** — multifidus atrophy and transversus abdominis inhibition are common findings. Motor control retraining (McGill approach) targets these structures.

**Athletic pubalgia (sports hernia)** — adductor/abdominal aponeurosis strain at pubic symphysis. Common in ice hockey, soccer, rugby.

## Training Recommendations

### Evidence-Based Core Training

**Stability before strength:**
- McGill big three: curl-up, side bridge, bird-dog
- Pallof press for anti-rotation
- Dead bug for anti-extension

**Load bearing:**
- Loaded carries (farmers walk, suitcase carry): 3-5 sets, 20-40m
- Chops and lifts: diagonal patterns integrating rotation
- Jefferson curls: flexion tolerance and erector endurance

**Rotational power:**
- Med ball slams: 3×8-10 for power
- Cable woodchops: 3×10-12 for rotational strength
- Rotational medicine ball throws

### Running-Specific Core

- **Frequency:** 2-3×/week dedicated core for runners
- **Focus:** Anti-extension (plank variations) and lateral stability (侧桥)
- **Duration:** 20-30 min sessions; higher frequency lower volume
- **Avoid:** Excessive crunches — flexion under load increases disc stress

### Back Pain Prevention

- **McGill curl-up:** 3×10, legs bent, hands under lumbar spine
- **Side bridge:** 3×20-30s per side; progress to variations
- **Bird-dog:** 3×8-10 per side; focus on stable spine
- **Cat-camel:** 2×10 for thoracic mobility
