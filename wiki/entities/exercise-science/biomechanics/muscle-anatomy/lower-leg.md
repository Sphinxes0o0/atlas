# Lower Leg — Calf, Tibialis Anterior & Deep Compartment

## Anatomy Overview

### Gastrocnemius

**Origin:** Medial head — posterior medial femoral condyle; Lateral head — posterior lateral femoral condyle

**Insertion:** Calcaneus via Achilles tendon

**Innervation:** Tibial nerve (S1-S2)

Two-headed muscle crossing both knee and ankle. Powerful plantar flexor (walking, running, jumping). Active in push-off phase of running. Crosses knee → knee flexion component. [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/thigh]] (biceps femoris also crosses knee — both involved in knee flexion).

### Soleus

**Origin:** Posterior tibia (soleal line), fibular head

**Insertion:** Calcaneus via Achilles tendon

**Innervation:** Tibial nerve (S1-S2)

Single-joint calf muscle (does NOT cross knee). Critical for standing posture and running economy. Higher activation than gastrocnemius during running at moderate speeds. [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/core]] (soleus functions analogously to transversus abdominis as anti-movement stabilizer).

### Tibialis Anterior

**Origin:** Lateral tibia, interosseous membrane

**Insertion:** Medial cuneiform, base of 1st metatarsal

**Innervation:** Deep peroneal nerve (L4-S1)

Primary dorsiflexor. Eccentrically controls plantarflexion during running gait (heel strike to midstance). [[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/thigh]] (tibialis anterior weakness alters running biomechanics and increases knee injury risk).

### Peroneal Group (Peroneus Longus & Brevis)

**Peroneus Longus:** Origin — fibular head, proximal 2/3 lateral fibula; Insertion — medial cuneiform, base 1st metatarsal. Foot eversion + plantarflexion; stabilizes lateral ankle.

**Peroneus Brevis:** Origin — distal 2/3 lateral fibula; Insertion — base 5th metatarsal. Primary evertor; lateral ankle stabilizer.

**Innervation:** Superficial peroneal nerve (L5-S1).

[[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/hip]] (peroneal muscles share L5-S1 innervation with hip abductors — gluteus medius weakness increases peroneal demand).

### Deep Posterior Compartment

**Tibialis Posterior:** Origin — interosseous membrane, tibia, fibula; Insertion — navicular, cuneiforms, metatarsals 2-4. Primary invertor + plantarflexor. Critical arch stabilizer. Innervation: Tibial nerve (L4-S1).

**Flexor Hallucis Longus (FHL):** Origin — fibula, interosseous membrane; Insertion — distal phalanx of great toe. Push-off during running/gait. Innervation: Tibial nerve (L5-S2).

**Flexor Digitorum Longus (FDL):** Origin — posterior tibia; Insertion — distal phalanges 2-5. Toe flexion during push-off. Innervation: Tibial nerve (L5-S2).

[[entities/exercise-science/biomechanics/muscle-anatomy/chest-back]] (FHL shares the "big toe push-off" function with latissimus dorsi in the posterior chain during running).

## Function in Exercise & Running

### Calf Muscle EMG During Key Exercises

| Exercise | Gastrocnemius | Soleus |
|----------|--------------|--------|
| Standing calf raise | 70-90% MVIC | 50-70% MVIC |
| Seated calf raise | 30-50% MVIC | 80-100% MVIC |
| Running (push-off) | 60-80% MVIC | 70-90% MVIC |
| Jumping | 80-100% MVIC | 60-80% MVIC |
| Walking | 40-60% MVIC | 50-70% MVIC |

### Tibialis Anterior in Running

- **Initial contact:** Concentric contraction to lift toes (dorsiflexion)
- **Midstance:** Eccentric lowering of forefoot (controlled plantarflexion)
- **Swing phase:** Maintains foot clearance (dorsiflexion)
- **Activation pattern:** Pre-activates before foot strike for anticipatory control

### Ankle Strategy in Balance

1. **Long peroneal nerve (L5) dominant** — dorsiflexion and toe extension
2. **Tibial nerve (S1) dominant** — plantarflexion and toe flexion
3. **Imbalance** — L5 > S1 → foot drop; S1 > L5 → calf dominance

### Lower Leg Running Mechanics

- **Push-off:** Gastrocnemius and soleus generate propulsive force via Achilles tendon
- **Elastic energy:** Achilles tendon stores ~35% energy during stance; returns during push-off
- **Arch function:** Tibialis posterior supports medial longitudinal arch during loading
- **Balance:** Peroneals prevent lateral ankle sprain during uneven terrain

## Common Injuries

| Structure | Injury | Mechanism |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Gastrocnemius | Strain / tennis leg | Sudden dorsiflexion stretch; pushing off; older athletes |
| Soleus | Strain | Low-speed running; excessive mileage; deep calf |
| Achilles tendon | Tendinopathy | Overload; hill running; tight calf; 30-50y athletes |
| Tibialis anterior | Shin splints (MTSS) | Overuse; new runners; hard surfaces |
| Peroneals | Peroneal tendinopathy | Inversion stress; lateral ankle instability |
| Tibialis posterior | Posterior tibial tendinopathy | Overuse; fallen arch; running |
| FHL | FHL tendinopathy | Hallux rigidus; running; ballet |
| Deep compartment | Chronic compartment syndrome | Exercise-induced pain; tightness; fascial restriction |

**Achilles tendinopathy** — insertional (at calcaneus) vs mid-substance. Associated with excessive calf tightness, HSR training, and increased training volume.

**Medial tibial stress syndrome (shin splints)** — tibialis anterior overuse along medial tibial border. Common in new runners and those who increased mileage rapidly.

**Chronic exertional compartment syndrome** — elevated compartment pressure during exercise causes pain and neurological symptoms. Fasciotomy is definitive treatment.

**Peroneal tendon subluxation** — peroneal tendons displace over lateral malleolus due to superior peroneal retinaculum tear. Often associated with ankle instability.

**Plantar fasciitis** — tibialis posterior and intrinsic foot muscles weakness → arch collapse → fascial insertion strain at calcaneus.

## Training Recommendations

### Calf Strengthening

**Standing calf raise** → gastrocnemius emphasis
**Seated calf raise** → soleus emphasis (knee flexed, reduces gastrocnemius contribution)

- **Frequency:** 2-3×/week for running
- **Volume:** 3-5 sets × 12-20 reps (hypertrophy); 3-5 sets × 6-10 (strength)
- **Eccentric emphasis:** Single-leg calf drops for Achilles health
- **Progression:** Load → reps → speed → plyometric

### Tibialis Anterior Training

- **Dorsiflexion with band:** 3×15-20; light resistance
- **Toe raises:** Bodyweight 3×20-30; hold at top
- **Running mechanic drills:** Skipping, high-knees, foot taps
- **Eccentric focus:** Slow lowering from dorsiflexed position

### Deep Posterior Compartment

- **Inversion against band:** Tibialis posterior; 3×12-15
- **Short foot exercise:** Intrinsic foot strengthening; arch maintenance
- **Toe yoga:** Great toe and lesser toe flexion/extension against resistance

### Ankle Stability for Runners

- **Alphabet ankle:** 2×ABC in air — joint mobility + strength
- **Single-leg balance:** 3×30-60s per side; progress on unstable surface
- **Eversion strengthening:** Peroneal band work; 3×15 per side
- **Intrinsic foot:** Towel scrunches, marble pickups
