# Hamstrings Function (腘绳肌功能)

## Overview

The hamstrings are a group of three muscles located on the posterior thigh, functioning as both hip extensors and knee flexors. They play a critical role in deceleration during running, power generation for sprinting, and posterior chain integrity during jumping and landing.

## Muscle Components

| Muscle | Innervation | Primary Actions |
|--------|-------------|----------------|
| [[entities/exercise-science/physiology/biceps-femoris]] (long & short head) | Sciatic nerve (tibial division) | Hip extension, knee flexion, lateral rotation |
| [[entities/exercise-science/physiology/semimembranosus]] | Sciatic nerve (tibial division) | Hip extension, knee flexion, medial rotation |
| [[entities/exercise-science/physiology/semitendinosus]] | Sciatic nerve (tibial division) | Hip extension, knee flexion, medial rotation |

## Architecture & Function

- **Biceps femoris long head**: Crosses both hip and knee; most vulnerable to strain injury due to long length
- **Biceps femoris short head**: Only crosses knee; purely a knee flexor
- **Semimembranosus**: Largest cross-sectional area; complex distal attachment to posterior medial tibia
- **Semitendinosus**: Distinct long tendon (~25-30cm); commonly used as ACL graft source

## Biomechanics

### Eccentric Function
Hamstrings act as "brakes" during running, controlling knee extension momentum in terminal swing phase. Elite sprinters generate hamstring forces 2-3x body weight during late swing.

### Stance Phase
- Hip extension powered by gluteus maximus and hamstrings
- Knee flexion stabilizes tibia during mid-stance

## Training Considerations

### Key Exercises
- **Nordic hamstring curl**: Eccentric-only; most evidence for injury prevention
- **Romanian deadlift**: Hip-dominant hamstring emphasis; lumbar load requires attention
- **Hip thrust**: Glute-hamstring integration; hip extension focus
- **Leg curl**: Isolates knee flexion; useful for reinnervation post-injury

### Sprint-Specific
- **Bounding**: Plyometric hip extension power
- **Resisted sprints**: Eccentric overload at high velocities

## Injury Prevention

- **Eccentric training**: Nordic curls 2-3x/week reduces strain risk by 50-65%
- **Rate of force development**: Explosive decelerations require specific velocity training
- **Fatigue management**: Most injuries occur in late matches/training when fatigue accumulates

## Common Injuries

- **Proximal hamstring tendinopathy**: Insertion at ischial tuberosity; running and cycling
- **Hamstring strain**: Most common in biceps femoris long head; eccentric overload mechanism
- **Avulsion injuries**: Proximal origin from ischial tuberosity; high-speed running
