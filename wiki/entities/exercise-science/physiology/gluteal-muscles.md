# Gluteal Muscles (臀肌功能)

## Overview

The gluteal muscle group consists of three muscles (gluteus maximus, medius, and minimus) that form the posterior hip musculature. As the largest and most powerful muscle group in the body, the glutes are essential for hip extension, abduction, and stabilization during weight-bearing activities.

## Muscle Components

| Muscle | Innervation | Primary Actions |
|--------|-------------|----------------|
| [[entities/exercise-science/physiology/gluteus-maximus]] | Inferior gluteal nerve (L5-S2) | Hip extension, external rotation, adduction |
| [[entities/exercise-science/physiology/gluteus-medius]] | Superior gluteal nerve | Hip abduction, medial rotation of femur |
| [[entities/exercise-science/physiology/gluteus-minimus]] | Superior gluteal nerve | Hip abduction, medial rotation (assist) |

## Architecture & Function

### Gluteus Maximus
- Largest muscle by mass (~60% Type II fibers in elite power athletes)
- Primary hip extensor; key for sprinting, climbing, rising from squat
- Upper fibers contribute to abduction; lower fibers to adduction
- Critical for IT-band tensioning via iliotibial band attachment

### Gluteus Medius
- Fan-shaped muscle with anterior, middle, and posterior fibers
- Anterior fibers: Medial rotation + hip flexion
- Middle fibers: Pure abduction
- Posterior fibers: Lateral rotation + extension
- Essential for frontal plane pelvic stability during single-leg stance

### Gluteus Minimus
- Smallest glute; deep to gluteus medius
- Assist medius in abduction and medial rotation
- Often underrepresented in rehabilitation programs

## Clinical Significance

### Gluteal Inhibition
- Prolonged sitting → gluteus maximus inhibition → compensatory overactivity in erector spinae
- Hip flexor tightness → gluteal activation deficits
- Knee valgus during squat → gluteus medius weakness

### Running Gait Implications
- Gluteus medius insufficiency → contralateral pelvic drop → increased knee valgus
-链接 to IT-band syndrome, patellofemoral pain, and hip osteoarthritis

## Training Considerations

### Gluteus Maximus Emphasis
- **Hip thrust**: Direct gluteal activation; 1RM hip thrust correlates with sprint performance
- **Back squat**: Significant glute involvement at parallel and below
- **Romanian deadlift**: Hip-hinge pattern; glutes and hamstrings
- **Bulgarian split squat**: Unilateral loading reveals glute activation asymmetries

### Gluteus Medius Emphasis
- **Side-lying clam**: Entry-level medius activation
- **Lateral band walks**: Frontal plane hip strengthening
- **Single-leg squat**: Requires significant medius stability
- **Side planks with hip abduction**: Combined stability + strength

## Common Injuries

- **Piriformis syndrome**: Gluteal region pain; often misdiagnosed as sciatica
- **IT-band syndrome**: Gluteus maximus/TFL contribution to IT-band tension
- **Greater trochanteric pain syndrome**: Gluteus medius/minimus tendinopathy
