# Lower Leg Muscles (小腿与足部肌群)

## Overview

The lower leg musculature powers plantarflexion and dorsiflexion, controls tibial rotation, and maintains foot arch integrity. These muscles are critical for running economy, jumping power, and gait stability throughout the stance phase.

## Calf Muscle Architecture

### Gastrocnemius
- Bipennate structure; two heads with distinct origins
- Crosses both knee and ankle; active in bent-knee plantarflexion
- Fast-twitch dominant (~50% Type II); critical for jumping and sprinting

### Soleus
- Unipennate; lies deep to gastrocnemius
- Crosses only ankle; soleus active in straight-knee positions
- Slow-twitch dominant (~70% Type I); postural function
- Called "second heart" for venous return assistance during walking

### Training Implications
- **Standing calf raise**: Gastrocnemius emphasis (knee extended)
- **Seated calf raise**: Soleus emphasis (knee flexed)
- **Single-leg calf raise**: Unilateral loading; identifies asymmetries

## Tibialis Anterior

- Primary dorsiflexor; prevents foot slap during swing phase of gait
- Eccentrically decelerates tibial advancement during downhill running
- Weakness causes foot drop and increased trip risk

## Deep Calf Muscles

- **Tibialis posterior**: Supports medial longitudinal arch; spring function
- **Flexor hallucis longus**: Hallux (big toe) flexion; push-off power
- **Flexor digitorum longus**: Toe flexion for propulsion

## Common Injuries

- **Achilles tendinopathy**: Mid-portion or insertional; gastrocnemius-soleus overuse
- **Calf strain**: Gastrocnemius high-speed running; eccentric demand
- **Tibialis anterior syndrome**: Anterior compartment hypertension
- **Plantar fasciitis**: Tibialis posterior and intrinsic foot muscle dysfunction
- **Peroneal tendinopathy**: Lateral ankle instability sequelae

## Hip Region Injuries

- **Piriformis syndrome**: Buttock pain with sciatic nerve symptoms
- **Iliopsoas strain/tendinopathy**: Hip flexor pain; running and kicking
- **Adductor strain**: Common in hockey and soccer; rapid direction change
- **IT-band syndrome**: TFL/gluteus medius contribution to lateral knee pain
