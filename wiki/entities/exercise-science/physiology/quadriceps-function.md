# Quadriceps Function (股四头肌功能)

## Overview

The quadriceps femoris is the largest muscle group in the human body, consisting of four muscles that converge to form the patellar tendon. As the primary knee extensor, it plays a critical role in locomotion, squatting, and virtually all weight-bearing activities.

## Muscle Components

| Muscle | Innervation | Primary Actions |
|--------|-------------|----------------|
| [[entities/exercise-science/physiology/rectus-femoris]] | Femoral nerve (L2-L4) | Hip flexion + knee extension (biarticular) |
| [[entities/exercise-science/physiology/vastus-lateralis]] | Femoral nerve | Knee extension (largest Q angle contribution) |
| [[entities/exercise-science/physiology/vastus-medialis]] | Femoral nerve | Knee extension (VMO controls patellar tracking) |
| [[entities/exercise-science/physiology/vastus-intermedius]] | Femoral nerve | Knee extension (deepest portion) |

## Architecture & Function

- **Rectus femoris**: Only quadriceps muscle crossing both hip and knee — contributes to hip flexion during knee extension movements
- **Vastus lateralis**: Largest component (~40% of quad mass), primary contributor to lateral patellar tracking
- **Vastus medialis oblique (VMO)**: Distal portion specifically controls medial patellar displacement; often atrophied post-injury
- **Vastus intermedius**: Deepest layer, purely knee-extending

## Training Considerations

### Compound Movements
- **Back squat**: Primary quad developer; depth affects rectus femoris lengthening
- **Leg press**: Safer alternative for spinal loading; can target VMO with foot placement
- **Lunges**: Unilateral loading reveals asymmetries; rectus femoris emphasized with longer stride

### Isolation Exercises
- **Terminal knee extensions**: VMO isolation at full extension
- **Leg extension**: Quadriceps isolation; avoid excessive load at end-range to protect patellofemoral joint

## Common Injuries

- **Patellar tendinopathy**: Overuse from repetitive jumping/landing; VMO weakness implicated
- **Quadriceps strain**: Common in sprinters; rectus femoris most frequently affected
- **Patellofemoral pain syndrome**: VMO timing/strength deficits alter patellar tracking
