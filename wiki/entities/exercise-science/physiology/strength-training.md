# Strength Training (力量训练)

## Overview

Strength training encompasses resistance training modalities designed to improve force production, rate of force development, and neuromuscular efficiency. It is a cornerstone of athletic development and rehabilitation across the lifespan.

## Strength Qualities

### Maximal Strength
- **1RM**: Maximum load lifted with good form
- **Neural adaptations**: Motor unit recruitment, firing frequency, synchronization
- **Cross-education**: Unilateral training improves contralateral limb strength

### Power
- **Rate of force development (RFD)**: Force/time gradient; critical for athletic performance
- **Force-velocity relationship**: Higher velocity = lower force; optimal power at 30-60% 1RM
- **Applications**: Olympic lifts, plyometrics, sprinting

### Strength Endurance
- **Time under tension**: >60s continuous contraction reduces strength gains
- **Hypertrophic endurance**: 8-15 rep ranges for metabolic stress
- **Context**: Sport-specific strength maintenance; rehabilitation

## Mechanisms of Strength Adaptation

### Neural Adaptations (Early Training)
- Increased motor unit recruitment (50% of early strength gains)
- Enhanced firing frequency (rate coding)
- Reduced antagonist co-activation
- Improved intramuscular coordination

### Morphological Adaptations (Later Training)
- **Hypertrophy**: Muscle fiber enlargement; 8-12 week timeline for noticeable changes
- **Pennation angle changes**: Increased muscle attachment to tendon
- **Tendon stiffness**: Improved series elastic element transmission

### Hormonal Responses
- Testosterone: Acute increase with heavy training; role in hypertrophy debated
- Growth hormone: High-volume training; metabolic stress mediator
- Cortisol: Catabolic; excessive volume counterproductive

## Resistance Training Methods

### Mechanical Tension
- Primary driver of hypertrophy
- Load: 65-85% 1RM for strength/hypertrophy
- Progressive overload essential

### Metabolic Stress
- **Time under tension**: 40-70s per set
- **Rest periods**: 60-90s for hypertrophy; 2-5min for strength
- **Blood flow restriction**: Low-load training with venous occlusion

### Muscle Damage
- **Eccentric focus**: Lengthened muscle contractions
- **DOMS**: Delayed onset muscle soreness; adaptation signal
- **Novel stimuli**: New angles/exercises maximize damage response

## Periodization Models

### Linear Periodization
- Progressive load increase with decreasing volume
- Traditional: mesocycle → macrocycle progression
- Effective for novice to intermediate

### Undulating Periodization
- Daily or weekly variation in intensity/volume
- Prevents accommodation; maintains anabolic stimulus
- Evidence supports superior hypertrophy outcomes

### Block Periodization
- Accumulation → Transmutation → Realization phases
- Concentrated training blocks for specific qualities
- Elite athlete application

## Exercise Selection Principles

### Compound vs Isolation
- **Compound**: Squat, deadlift, bench, row — multi-joint, high neural demand
- **Isolation**: Leg extension, bicep curl — single-joint, targeted hypertrophy

### Movement Patterns
- Push (horizontal/vertical), pull (horizontal/vertical), squat, hinge, carry
- Balanced programming prevents postural imbalances
