# Core Muscles (核心肌群功能)

## Overview

The core musculature encompasses muscles of the trunk that transmit force between upper and lower extremities, maintain spinal stability, and enable efficient force transfer during movement. The core functions as a kinetic mediator rather than a prime mover in most athletic movements.

### Deep Core / Local Stabilizers

| Muscle | Innervation | Primary Function |

## Deep Core System

### Transversus Abdominis
- deepest abdominal muscle with horizontal fiber orientation
- Forms "corset" around spine; activated anticipatorily before limb movement
- Dysfunction linked to low back pain; often inhibited in chronic LBP populations

### Deep Core Muscles
- **Multifidus**: Segmental paraspinal; provides proprioceptive feedback and segmental stability
- **Quadratus lumborum**: Lateral spinal stabilization; hip-hinge mechanics
- **Internal oblique**: Force couple with external oblique for rotational control

## Abdominal Wall Architecture

### Rectus Abdominis
- Segmental innervation; upper/lower portions activated differently
- Primary function is compression, not spinal flexion against resistance
- "Six-pack" appearance unrelated to functional capacity

### Oblique System
- **External oblique**: Same-side rotation (e.g., right external = right rotation)
- **Internal oblique**: Opposite-side rotation (e.g., right internal = right rotation)
- Creates diagonal force transmission for rotational sports

## Training Considerations

### Stability-First Approach
- **Plank variations**: Global core endurance; avoid spinal extension
- **Dead bug**: Anti-extension pattern; transversus activation
- **Pallof press**: Anti-rotation; rotational stability
- **Bird dog**: Anti-extension + limb movement coordination

### Functional Integration
- **Chopping/lifting patterns**: Diagonal force transfer
- **Turkish get-up**: Full-spectrum core stability under load
- **Carries**: Farmer's walk, suitcase carry for lateral stability

## Core-Injury Relationships

- **Low back pain**: Transversus and multifidus inhibition common in chronic LBP
- ** athletic hernia**: External oblique/internal oblique separation at inguinal canal
- **Diastasis recti**: Linea alba separation; transversus dysfunction
