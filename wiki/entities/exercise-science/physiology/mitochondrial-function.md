# Mitochondrial Function & Bioenergetics (线粒体功能)

## Overview

Mitochondria are the primary site of aerobic energy production in skeletal muscle. Exercise induces profound mitochondrial adaptations through multiple signaling pathways, primarily centered on PGC-1α, AMPK, and SIRT1.

## Key Mechanism: PGC-1α Pathway

```
Exercise → AMPK activation + Ca²⁺ signaling + NAD⁺ increase
        ↓
SIRT1 activation (NAD⁺-dependent)
        ↓
PGC-1α deacetylation/phosphorylation
        ↓
NRF-1/NRF-2 activation → TFAM
        ↓
Mitochondrial biogenesis (↑mtDNA, ↑citrate synthase)
```

## Exercise-Induced Mitochondrial Adaptations

| Adaptation | Effect | Evidence |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| PGC-1α mRNA | +3.2× at 85% VO2max | J Appl Physiol 2024 |
| mtDNA content | +34% after 6 weeks HIIT | Zhang 2024 |
| Complex I activity | +28% after 12 weeks endurance | Kang 2025 |
| Complex IV activity | +37% after 12 weeks | Kang 2025 |
| Max coupled respiration | +31% after 12 weeks | Kang 2025 |

## ETC Complexes & Training

| Complex | Function | Training Effect |
|---------|---------|----------------|
| Complex I (NADH dehydrogenase) | Electron entry from NADH | +28% with endurance training |
| Complex II (Succinate dehydrogenase) | Electron entry from FADH2 | +22% with endurance training |
| Complex III (Cytochrome bc1) | Electron transfer | +19% improvement |
| Complex IV (Cytochrome c oxidase) | Terminal electron transfer | +37% (most pronounced) |
| Complex V (ATP synthase) | Proton gradient → ATP | Coupled respiration +31% |

## NAD+ and Mitochondrial Health

NAD+ declines ~10-15% per decade with aging.

| Intervention | Effect on Mitochondrial Function |
|--------------|--------------------------------|
| NMN (500mg/day) + exercise | ATP production +42% vs control |
| NR (300mg×2/day) + exercise | Complex I activity +31% |
| Exercise alone | PGC-1α activation, SIRT1 activation |

**Synergy**: NAD+ precursors + exercise produce superior outcomes vs either alone.

## Mitophagy & Quality Control

Exercise activates mitophagy — the selective autophagy of damaged mitochondria. This maintains a healthy mitochondrial population through:

- Removal of dysfunctional mitochondria (via Parkin/PINK1 pathway)
- Biogenesis of new, functional mitochondria
- Dynamic balance between fusion and fission

## Limitations

- **Over training risk**: Excessive training → oxidative damage, mtDNA mutations, electron leak, reduced function
- **Age factor**: Mitochondrial dysfunction in aging — exercise can partially reverse
- **Individual variation**: Training response varies significantly based on baseline mitochondrial function
