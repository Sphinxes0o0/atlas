# Exercise Immunology (运动免疫学)

## Overview

Exercise immunology examines how physical activity affects immune function. The relationship between exercise intensity and infection risk follows a **J-shaped curve** — moderate exercise enhances immunity, while intensive training can temporarily suppress it.

## J-Shaped Curve

| Exercise Level | URTI Risk | Effect |
|--------------|-----------|--------|
| Sedentary | Baseline | — |
| Moderate (30-60 min/day) | ↓ 20-50% | **Enhanced immune surveillance** |
| High intensity | ↑ elevated | Potential suppression |
| Marathon/Ultra | ↑ 100-500% | Significant suppression |

## "Open Window" Mechanism

Following intense exercise (>90 min at 55-75% VO2max), immune function is temporarily suppressed for **3-72 hours**:

| Mechanism | Effect |
|-----------|--------|
| Catecholamine surge | Epinephrine/norepinephrine spike |
| Lymphocyte trafficking | Mobilization during → lymphopenia post |
| Blood flow redistribution | Away from lymphoid organs |
| Cytokine perturbation | Pro-inflammatory → suppressed |

## Evidence

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Single bout >90 min | Measurable immune suppression for 3-72h |
| Marathon/ultra | URTI risk ↑ 100-500% in weeks after |
| Moderate daily exercise | URTI risk ↓ 20-50% vs sedentary |

## Key Considerations

- **Confounders**: Sleep, nutrition, stress, travel often not controlled
- **Individual variation**: Substantial
- **Open window controversy**: Direct infection incidence correlation is inconsistent
- **Training periodization**: Key strategy to minimize immune suppression

## Practical Recommendations

1. **Moderate exercise**: 30-60 min/day at moderate intensity for immune health
2. **Avoid high intensity during high-stress periods**
3. **Sleep optimization**: 7-9 hours
4. **Nutrition**: Adequate carb to maintain immune function
5. **Consider Glutamine** post-endurance event (infection risk ↓60-63%)
