# Arm Muscles (臂部肌群功能)

## Overview

The arm musculature primarily crosses the elbow joint, with secondary functions at the shoulder. The biceps, triceps, and brachialis are the primary elbow flexors and extensors, while forearm muscles control wrist, finger, and grip function.

## Muscle Components

### Elbow Flexors

| Muscle | Innervation | Primary Function |
|--------|-------------|------------------|
| [[entities/exercise-science/physiology/biceps-triceps]] | Musculocutaneous nerve | Elbow flexion, forearm supination, shoulder flexion |
| [[entities/exercise-science/physiology/brachialis]] | Musculocutaneous nerve | Pure elbow flexion (50% of flexion force) |
| [[entities/exercise-science/physiology/forearm-muscles]] | Median and radial nerves | Wrist flexion/extension, finger control, grip |

### Elbow Extensors

| Muscle | Innervation | Primary Function |
|--------|-------------|------------------|
| Triceps (long, lateral, medial heads) | Radial nerve | Elbow extension, shoulder extension (long head) |

## Biceps Brachii

### Two Heads
- **Long head**: Origin at supraglenoid tubercle; crosses shoulder; contributes to shoulder flexion
- **Short head**: Origin at coracoid process; purely elbow flexion contribution

### Functions
- Primary supinator of forearm (especially with elbow flexed)
- Secondary elbow flexor; brachialis provides 50% more force
- Long head stabilizes humeral head during overhead activities

### Training Notes
- Preacher curl: Eliminates shoulder involvement; isolates biceps
- Hammer curl: Emphasizes brachialis; neutral grip reduces biceps emphasis
- Cable supination: Isolates supinator function

## Triceps Brachii

### Three Heads
- **Long head**: Crosses shoulder and elbow; shoulder extension contribution
- **Lateral head**: Largest visible portion; pure elbow extension
- **Medial head**: Deepest; primary force in extended elbow positions

### Training Notes
- Overhead triceps extension: Long head stretch and activation
- Close-grip bench: Triceps emphasis with chest contribution
- Dips: Triceps dominant when torso upright

## Brachialis

- Lies deep to biceps; attaches to ulnar tuberosity
- Produces ~50% more elbow flexion force than biceps
- Does NOT produce supination (unlike biceps)
- Trained effectively with neutral-grip curls (hammer curls)

## Forearm Muscles

### Anterior Compartment (Flexors)
- Pronator teres, flexor carpi radialis, palmaris longus, flexor carpi ulnaris
- Grip-intensive sports show significant forearm development

### Posterior Compartment (Extensors)
- Extensor carpi radialis longus/brevis, extensor carpi ulnaris
- Often weak in individuals with computer/desk work

### Training Implications
- Wrist curls: Flexor emphasis
- Reverse wrist curls: Extensor emphasis (important for balance)
- Grip training: Farmer's walks, dead hangs for functional forearm endurance

## Common Injuries

- **Biceps tendinopathy**: Proximal long head; repetitive overhead
- **Triceps tendinopathy**: Olecranon insertion; common in throwing
- **Lateral epicondylitis**: Common extensor origin; forearm extensor dysfunction
- **Medial epicondylitis**: Pronator/flexor origin; golfer's elbow
- **Brachialis nerve entrapment**: Rare; causes weakness in elbow flexion
