# Shoulder Muscles (肩部肌群功能)

## Overview

The shoulder complex (glenohumeral joint) is the most mobile joint in the body, sacrificing stability for range of motion. Dynamic stability is provided by the rotator cuff and scapular stabilizers, while prime movers generate force for pushing and pulling movements.

## Muscle Components

### Prime Movers

| Muscle | Innervation | Primary Function |
|--------|-------------|------------------|
| [[entities/exercise-science/physiology/pectoralis-major]] | Medial/lateral pectoral nerves | Shoulder adduction, horizontal adduction, flexion |
| [[entities/exercise-science/physiology/deltoid-function]] | Axillary nerve (anterior/middle/posterior heads) | Shoulder abduction, flexion, extension |

### Dynamic Stabilizers

| Muscle | Innervation | Primary Function |
|--------|-------------|------------------|
| [[entities/exercise-science/physiology/rotator-cuff]] | Suprascapular, subscapular, axillary nerves | Glenohumeral compression, rotation |

## Deltoid Architecture

### Three Heads
- **Anterior deltoid**: Shoulder flexion, horizontal adduction; trained in all pressing movements
- **Middle deltoid**: Primary shoulder abductor (15-90°); critical for overhead work
- **Posterior deltoid**: Shoulder extension, horizontal abduction; often underdeveloped

### Training Implications
- Lateral raises primarily target middle deltoid
- Overhead pressing loads anterior deltoid significantly
- Rear delt flyes address posterior deltoid weakness common in desk workers

## Rotator Cuff Function

Four muscles forming a "cuff" around the humeral head:

| Muscle | Primary Action | Clinical Relevance |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|
| Supraspinatus | Abduction initiation (0-15°) | Most commonly torn; impingement zone |
| Infraspinatus | External rotation | Shoulder power in throwing |
| Teres minor | External rotation, adduction | Part of rotator cuff |
| Subscapularis | Internal rotation | Impingement with subscapularis tightness |

The rotator cuff maintains glenohumeral congruity by compressing the humeral head into the shallow glenoid socket during all shoulder movements.

## Pectoralis Major

Two heads with distinct functions:
- **Clavicular head**: Flexion (0-90°); active in forward flexion and incline pressing
- **Sternal head**: Adduction, extension from flexed position; dominant in decline pressing

Fiber orientation: Upper fibers (clavicular) contribute ~25% of total mass; lower fibers (sternal) ~75%.

## Common Injuries

- **Rotator cuff tendinopathy**: Degenerative changes in supraspinatus most common
- **Impingement syndrome**: Subacromial bursa compression; often secondary to cuff weakness
- **Pectoralis major tear**: Uncommon; usually with bench press eccentric loading
- **SLAP lesions**: Superior labrum anterior-posterior tears; overhead athletes

## Training Considerations

### Overhead Pressing
- Military press: Anterior + middle deltoid emphasis
- Handstand push-up: Significant anterior deltoid and triceps demand
- Push press: Lower body drive reduces shoulder load

### Pulling Patterns
- **Horizontal rows**: Posterior deltoid + mid trapezius + rhomboids
- **Vertical pulls**: Lat + posterior deltoid
- **Face pulls**: Rotator cuff endurance, rear deltoid

### Cuff Isolation
- **External rotation**: Infraspinatus and teres minor
- **Internal rotation**: Subscapularis (often overtrained relative to external rotators)
- **Sidelying external rotation**: Most isolated rotator cuff exercise
