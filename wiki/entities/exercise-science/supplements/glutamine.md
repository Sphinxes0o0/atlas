# Glutamine (谷氨酰胺)

## Overview

Glutamine (Gln) is the most abundant amino acid in plasma (~20% of free amino acid pool) and skeletal muscle (~60% of intracellular amino acids). It is a "conditionally essential" amino acid — normally synthesized in sufficient quantities, but becomes critical during stress states (intense exercise, trauma, infection, illness).

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Plasma concentration | 0.6-0.9 mmol/L |
| Muscle intracellular | 2-20 mmol/L |
| Primary storage | Skeletal muscle (~75% of body reserves) |
| Key organs | Muscle (storage), gut (consumption), immune cells (consumption) |

## Key Mechanisms

### Nitrogen Metabolism
- Glutamine carries **2 nitrogen atoms** per molecule — the body's primary nitrogen shuttle
- Supports purine/pyrimidine synthesis for rapidly dividing cells

### Glutathione Precursor
- Glutamine → glutamate → glutathione (key cellular antioxidant)
- Critical for protecting against exercise-induced oxidative stress

### Gut Barrier Support
- Intestinal epithelial cells use glutamine as primary fuel
- Supports gut barrier integrity, may reduce "leaky gut" during exercise

### Immune Function
- Immune cells (lymphocytes, macrophages) have extremely high glutamine utilization
- Post-exercise "immune window" (24-48h) coincides with glutamine recovery

## Evidence Summary

| Application | Evidence | Finding |
|------------|----------|---------|
| Post-marathon infection prevention | **Strong** | 2.5g post-race → infection risk ↓60-63% (Castell 2002) |
| Gut barrier function | Moderate | ≥30g/day may improve barrier; doses achievable in studies only |
| Strength recovery/DOMS | Moderate | 0.3g/kg lean body mass → faster recovery, less soreness |
| General athletic performance | Limited | No significant performance enhancement in healthy athletes |

## Dosing

| Scenario | Dose | Timing |
|----------|------|--------|
| Post-endurance event | 2.5g | Within 2h post-race |
| Gut barrier support | 0.3 g/kg lean mass | Before/during intense training |
| General recovery | 5-10g | Post-workout |

## Marathon/Infection Evidence

Castell et al. (2002) demonstrated that marathon runners supplementing 2.5g glutamine immediately post-race and 2 hours later showed infection rates of **8%** vs **26%** in placebo group over 7 days post-race (p<0.05). This is the strongest evidence for glutamine in exercise nutrition.

## Limitations

- Performance enhancement in healthy athletes: **minimal evidence**
- Doses for gut barrier improvement (≥30g/day) are high and impractical
- Tumor cell metabolism concern: some worry about providing fuel to potential cancer cells (controversial, not established)
- Long-term high-dose safety not well established
